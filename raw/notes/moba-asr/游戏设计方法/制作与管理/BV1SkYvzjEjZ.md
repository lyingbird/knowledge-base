Ladies and gentlemen, thank you for attending today's session. Over the past years, our team has safeguarded hundreds of games facing a wide range of security challenges and threats along the way. Through these experiences, we have not only encountered risks, but also developed practical solutions. Today, I will share some key insights and lessons learned from our journey in game security. This presentation will focus on three dimensions: one, an overview of the current global game security ecosystem; two, a deep dive into three core risks areas; three, the construction of a global game security defense system. Now, let's examine the challenges and trends shaping the industry. According to New2 and the game industry, the global game market revenue in 2024 was broken to reach 184.3 billion dollars, with player base exceeding 3.42 billion, meaning one out of every two people worldwide is a gamer. However, beneath this booming industry, security threats are certainly eroding its foundation. I have compiled records of game security incidents reported by media outlets across different regions since 2024. The data reveals that security breaches are currently significantly impacting game reputation and player trust. A survey conducted by us revealed that 80% of players consider security a key factor when choosing a game. Furthermore, 80% of players reported that the presence of cheats reduced their willingness to spend money on games. Even more striking, every individual gaming company has listed security as its top priority. These findings underscore one inevitable truth: game security is more critical than ever. Next, let's structure the key security risks that games face today. The security risks in online games are closely tied to game genres. For example, if a game's quality is poor on the client side, as in action or shooting games, the risk of cheating is significantly higher. Similarly, games with complex in-game economies face elevated risks such as fraud and currency manipulation. Understanding these risks allows us to develop targeted security measures that align with each game's unique challenges. While these specific risk factors may vary across game genres, all security threats ultimately fall into three critical categories: one, account security; two, fairness maintenance; three, infrastructure protection. Together, these three dimensions form the triangle of modern game security. Next, I will break down each of these risk categories in detail. The first major category of risks revolves around one of the most prevalent threats in gaming: account security breaches. There are several reasons why players lose access to their accounts. Hackers use different measures to break into accounts, including malware phishing, credential stuffing, account sharing, and social engineering. Combating accounts isn't going well. Hackers continuously refine their techniques, making it difficult to implement foolproof solutions. For globally operated games, these challenges become even more complex due to regional data regulations, cross-border hacking groups, and players generating fake security awareness. All of which significantly raise the bar for game companies' security strategies. If account security is the first line of defense for protecting players' digital identities, then game fairness is the cornerstone that sustains the integrity of gameplay. Now, let's turn our attention to the second category of game security risks: fairness-related threats. There are several scenarios that compromise game fairness, which we can categorize as follows: one, cheating; two, in-game economies risks; three, exploits; four, malicious behaviors. Last but not least, one of the most persistent threats to game fairness in recent years, as game companies worldwide have intensified their anti-cheat efforts. Cheaters developers have rapidly evolved their tactics. In the past, cheating tools directly modified the game's code and data. Today, however, hackers implement external modules that evade detection by operating outside traditional security boundaries. For example, mobile games now face collaboration hacks that operate at different layers, demand hardware and empower cheats. Certain PC games making detection significantly harder. Cheats already harm game fairness, but when combined with virtual economies, they become even more damaging. Modern cheats operations now leverage cloud phones and syncing devices to mass-produce in-game resources, leading to severe inflation. Revenue loss for game companies, player experiences degrade, reputational damage, even more concerning, these cheats systems have become large and well-organized, with advanced tools and strategies to survive, making the matter worse. Game exploits present another major challenge. These issues are especially difficult to manage due to their rapid spread and complex engineering and patching process. Meanwhile, behavioral fairness violations such as account stealing and intentionally losing continue to evolve alongside security measures. Each of these each of these threats damages game fairness in different ways, reinforcing the need for multi-layered defense strategies. The final category of security risks targets game infrastructure itself, with data attacks and marketing fraud being the two primary threats. Data attacks involve introduction, but did you know that 33% of all global data attacks in 2024 targeted the gaming industry? Attackers frequently strike during critical moments such as new game launches or major updates, aiming to disrupt operations and exploit game companies for small and mid-sized companies. The challenges are even greater without robust defenses. Game companies face serious disruption, financial losses, and reputational damage. Sometimes, pushing smaller companies out of business entirely. Apart from cyberattacks, marketing fraud is a hidden but harmful threat that many players promote results using both technical exploits and human deception. Fraudsters artificially inflate performance metrics, tricking companies into wasting millions on ineffective marketing campaigns. This forces companies into unreliable partnerships, reducing marketing effectiveness, harming ROI, and affecting overall game revenue. As gaming ecosystems become more interconnected and reliant on real-time services, infrastructure threats continue to develop. Both data attacks and marketing fraud represent multi-billion-dollar risks, demanding proactive measures to ensure operational stability and long-term profitability. Next, let's explore how we can build a global security defense system to combat these growing challenges to establish long-term security resilience. Game companies must develop two important capabilities: one, foundational protection; foundational protection involves building essential security barriers, including client-side protection, server-side integrity checks, and database security barriers. Two, continuous and proactive capabilities beyond foundational protection. Game security must be dynamic and adaptive. This includes intelligence gathering and threat analysis, real-time countermeasures, advanced user risk management, security system platforms, by using these active defense and adaptive gameplay companies can build security systems that reduce risks, game player trust, keep game running longer, and protect their earnings. Facing these facing these multi-dimensional security challenges, fragmented solutions are no longer viable. A two-dimensional strategy cannot rely on isolated experiences. It must evolve into a systematic security framework that game companies can apply across the entire development and operations lifecycle. These layered, consolidated, proven strategies enable companies to identify security risks and implement targeted countermeasures by integrating robust security frameworks and continuously adapting countermeasures. We can create a safer, fairer, and more sustainable gaming environment for everyone. In the era of globalization, game security is no longer optional; it is mission-critical and cornerstone of player trust and industry growth. Thank you.
